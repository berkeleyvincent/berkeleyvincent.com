Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) and High-Fidelity Digital Twins for Transient Thermal Fatigue in Reactor Auxiliary Heat Exchangers

Berkeley Vincent • Computational Mechanics, Thermal-Hydraulics & Asset Management Architecture
Executive Summary: Auxiliary heat exchangers and intermediate cooling loops in nuclear power plants operate under severe stochastic thermal cycling. Traditional Enterprise Asset Management (EAM) architectures compute fatigue damage via calendar operating hours or rainflow cycle-counting based on low-frequency thermocouple channels, systematically underestimating localized thermal stress. This paper formulates a real-time Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) digital twin framework that embeds transient conduction and Navier-Cauchy thermoelastic PDEs into neural network optimization, reconstructing interior thermal stress fields from sparse external telemetry to track low-cycle fatigue in real time.

1. The Deficit in Legacy Thermal Fatigue Asset Management

Critical pressure-boundary components in nuclear and heavy-process thermal loops experience aggressive transient thermal stresses:

  1. Thermal Attenuation & Phase Lag: External resistance temperature detectors (RTDs) lag internal fluid shock waves by 5 to 30 seconds due to the thermal diffusivity of heavy steel wall sections. Peak transient stresses occur before surface sensors register shifts.
  2. Thermal Stratification: In horizontal runs with low flow velocity, cold feedwater injection creates a sharp circumferential fluid layer interface. This produces severe localized bending moments and cyclic thermal striping that lumped-parameter models miss.
  3. Computational Latency: Full 3D transient computational fluid dynamics (CFD) coupled with thermoelastic finite element analysis (FEA) requires hours or days per transient event, making real-time digital twin monitoring impossible on edge hardware.

2. Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) Architecture

Rather than treating the asset twin as a pure empirical "black box," the PINN embeds the governing continuum partial differential equations (PDEs) directly into the backpropagation optimization process: 

+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  PHYSICS-INFORMED NEURAL TWIN TOPOLOGY                  |
|                                                                         |
|  Inputs: (r, theta, z, t) ──► [ Deep Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) ]     |
|                                      │                                  |
|                                      ├──► Predicted T(r, theta, z, t)   |
|                                      └──► Predicted sigma_ij(r, theta, t)|
|                                      │                                  |
|  Automatic Differentiation (Autograd)│                                  |
|  • dT/dt, d²T/dr²                    ▼                                  |
|  • PDE Residual Evaluation: L_physics = L_heat + L_thermoelastic        |
|  • Sensor Loss: L_data = || T_pred(r_o, t) - T_measured(t) ||²          |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+

Governing Continuum Equations

In cylindrical coordinates, transient heat conduction within an axisymmetric tube wall is governed by:

$$\rho c_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} - k \left[ \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left( r \frac{\partial T}{\partial r} \right) + \frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial z^2} \right] = 0$$

The resulting transient thermal strain generates thermoelastic stresses governed by the Navier-Cauchy equilibrium equations:

$$(1 - \nu) \left[ \frac{\partial^2 u_r}{\partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial u_r}{\partial r} - \frac{u_r}{r^2} \right] = \alpha (1 + \nu) \frac{\partial T}{\partial r}$$

Composite Loss Function

The PINN is trained by minimizing a composite loss function across space-time collocation points (\(N_{\text{coll}}\)) and sparse surface sensor measurements (\(N_{\text{sensor}}\)):

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = w_{\text{data}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + w_{\text{pde}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{pde}} + w_{\text{bc}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{bc}}$$

By leveraging Automatic Differentiation, the network computes exact partial derivatives without numerical discretization grids or mesh deformation issues.

3. Fatigue Life Formulation & Cumulative Damage Tracking

From the reconstructed stress tensor, the Von Mises equivalent stress range (\(\Delta\sigma_{\text{eq}}\)) is calculated. The allowable cycles to failure (\(N_f\)) under combined elastic-plastic strain range (\(\Delta\epsilon_{\text{total}}\)) follows the Coffin-Manson relation:

$$\frac{\Delta\epsilon_{\text{total}}}{2} = \frac{\sigma'_f}{E} (2N_f)^b + \epsilon'_f (2N_f)^c$$

The cumulative damage metric \(D(t)\) is continually integrated in the plant digital twin:

$$D(t) = \sum_{k=1}^M \frac{n_k}{N_{f,k}} \le 1.0$$

5. Conclusion

Static calendar-based maintenance and uncoupled structural calculations fail to capture the stochastic thermal fatigue that governs heat exchanger reliability in modern power infrastructure. By encoding partial differential equations of continuum thermoelasticity into neural networks, Physics-Informed Digital Twins turn surface temperature data into real-time, volumetric stress fields. This computational framework ensures structural integrity, maximizes plant uptime, and provides an unclassified, mathematically rigorous foundation for next-generation nuclear asset management.
