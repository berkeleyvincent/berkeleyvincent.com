Systems Engineering & Sovereign Sustainment

The Strategic Physics of Sovereign Resilience: Distributed Additive Manufacturing, Digital Thread Metallurgy, and High-Cadence Turnaround Architecture

Berkeley Vincent • Systems Engineering, Metallurgy & Industrial Sustainment
Executive Summary: Traditional defense-industrial sustainment relies on highly centralized domestic shipyards and extended trans-oceanic logistics pipelines. In contested maritime theaters, this model introduces fatal operational friction: component failures require months of transit and drydock queuing. This paper formulates an integrated, forward-deployed engineering architecture combining Directed Energy Deposition (DED) and Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) with in-situ melt-pool pyrometry and an immutable cryptographic Digital Thread. Modeling stochastic queue dynamics via Jackson network theory proves that point-of-need additive manufacturing reduces replacement lead times from 180+ days to under 72 hours while meeting ASME Section III/XI structural reliability standards.

1. Strategic Logistical Vulnerability & Theater Friction

Modern naval fleet sustainment faces severe vulnerabilities stemming from peacetime optimization:

  1. Lead-Time Asymmetry: Castings and specialized forgings for nickel-aluminum bronze (NAB) impellers, copper-nickel seawater valves, and high-strength alloy piping require lead times exceeding 12 to 24 months. Forward units cannot wait for global logistics links during high-intensity operations.
  2. Depot-Level Chokepoints: Geographic concentration of deep-maintenance shipyards creates single-point-of-failure risks. Kinetic or cyber disruptions against centralized drydocks halt fleet turnaround capabilities.
  3. The Qualification Barrier: Additive manufacturing has historically lacked real-time metallurgical certification. Pressure-retaining equipment cannot be installed without rigorous proof of microstructural integrity and absence of porosity.

2. Thermodynamics & Microstructural Kinetics of In-Situ Additive Repairs

Deploying metal additive systems (LPBF and Wire-DED) in expeditionary or tender-based environments demands precise control over localized thermal histories.

Rosenthal Moving Heat Source Model

The quasi-steady temperature field \(T(x,y,z)\) generated by a high-energy laser or arc source traversing a metallic substrate is formulated as:

$$T(x,y,z) - T_0 = \frac{\eta P}{2\pi k R} \exp\left( -\frac{v(R + \xi)}{2\alpha} \right)$$

Where \(P\) is beam power, \(\eta\) is absorptivity, \(v\) is scan velocity, \(\xi = x - vt\) tracks the moving coordinate axis, and \(R = \sqrt{\xi^2 + y^2 + z^2}\) represents the radial distance to the melt pool.

Solidification Morphology & Cooling Rate Control

The grain structure transition between columnar dendritic and equiaxed crystals—which governs whether a component exhibits isotropic toughness or directional weakness—is controlled by the ratio of the thermal gradient \(G\) to growth rate \(R_s\):

$$\dot{T} = G \cdot R_s \quad (\text{K/s})$$

Maintaining cooling rates \(\dot{T}\) between \(10^3\text{--}10^5\text{ K/s}\) prevents deleterious phase transformations (such as embrittling sigma phases in duplex stainless steels or aluminum-rich intermetallics in bronze alloys).

4. Operational Turnaround Modeling: Jackson Queuing Theory

Component sustainment across theater repair hubs is mathematically modeled using an open Jackson queuing network. Total turnaround lead time in a legacy centralized system (\(W_{\text{central}}\)) includes round-trip inter-theater transit:

$$W_{\text{central}} = 2 \cdot T_{\text{transit}} + \frac{1}{\mu_c - \lambda}$$

In contrast, a distributed network with \(M\) forward point-of-need additive cells achieves:

$$W_{\text{dist}} = 2 \cdot T_{\text{local}} + \frac{1}{\mu_d - (\lambda / M)}$$

By eliminating the long transit term (\(T_{\text{transit}} \gg T_{\text{local}}\)) and spreading maintenance loads across parallel hubs, component replacement latency drops from hundreds of days to under 72 hours.

5. Conclusion

Sovereign operational resilience requires rethinking the logistics of maritime sustainment. By shifting from centralized foundries and vulnerable transit corridors to distributed, physics-qualified additive manufacturing cells governed by an encrypted digital thread, defense authorities convert maintenance from a strategic vulnerability into an operational deterrent.
