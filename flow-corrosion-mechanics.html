Multi-Mechanism Degradation in Coastal Secondary Cooling Circuits: Coupled Hydrodynamic Modeling of FAC and Marine Stress Corrosion Cracking

Berkeley Vincent • Materials Science, Fluid Dynamics & Plant Asset Integrity
Executive Summary: Secondary cooling loops and intermediate heat-rejection circuits in coastal marine environments operate under coupled mechanical, chemical, and atmospheric degradation regimes. Internally, high-velocity demineralized water circuits induce Flow-Accelerated Corrosion (FAC) by stripping protective magnetite (\(\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4\)) passivation layers. Externally, aerosolized chlorides drive Stress Corrosion Cracking (CISCC) and localized pitting across alloy boundaries. This paper develops a coupled multiphysics framework linking hydrodynamic wall shear stresses, electrochemical potential, and strain rates to predict wall thinning and establish continuous non-destructive condition monitoring.

1. The Dual-Vector Degradation Environment

Secondary-side fluid architectures in coastal industrial energy plants face simultaneous internal erosion-corrosion and external atmospheric attack:

  1. Internal Vector: Flow-Accelerated Corrosion (FAC): Under alkaline conditions, carbon steel forms a protective magnetite film (\(3\text{Fe} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + 4\text{H}_2\)). Between \(120^\circ\text{C}\) and \(180^\circ\text{C}\), turbulent eddies strip soluble ferrous ions (\(\text{Fe}^{2+}\)) from the interface, preventing film stabilization and causing continuous scalloped wall loss.
  2. External Vector: Chloride-Induced Stress Corrosion Cracking (CISCC): At relative humidity \(\text{RH} \ge 65\%\), deposited sea salt crystals form saturated chloride droplets on hot pipe surfaces (\(50^\circ\text{C}\text{--}95^\circ\text{C}\)). Residual tensile stresses from fabrication initiate transgranular or intergranular micro-fissures.

2. Mathematical Formulations & Degradation Kinetics

Internal Wall Thinning Rate (Kastner-Sanchez Formulation)

The rate of metal loss due to FAC (\(\dot{m}_\text{FAC}\), in \(\text{mm/year}\)) is governed by the convective mass transfer coefficient (\(k_m\)) and the iron concentration gradient:

$$\dot{m}_\text{FAC} = k_m \cdot (\theta_\text{eq} - \theta_\text{bulk}) \cdot f(T) \cdot f(\text{pH}) \cdot f(\text{DO}) \cdot f(\text{Cr})$$

Where \(k_m = 0.023 \cdot \frac{D_\text{diff}}{d} \cdot \text{Re}^{0.8} \cdot \text{Sc}^{0.33}\) derives from the classic Dittus-Boelter / Chilton-Colburn analogy for turbulent boundary layers, and \(f(\text{Cr}) = \frac{1}{1 + 1.25[\%\text{Cr}]^{1.4}}\) quantifies the exponential protection imparted by chromium alloying.

Hydrodynamic Wall Shear Stress

At pipe bends, reducers, and branch junctions, local flow separation generates peak wall shear stresses (\(\tau_w\)):

$$\tau_w = \frac{1}{2} C_f \cdot \rho \cdot v^2$$

Where turbulence intensity (\(I = u'/U\)) exceeds \(15\%\), mechanical wall shear strips the porous sub-micron oxide layer faster than repassivation kinetics can reform it, producing accelerated local thinning rates up to \(3.5\text{ mm/year}\).

CISCC Crack Growth Propagation Rate

Following pitting initiation under tensile hoop stress (\(\sigma_h\)), the crack propagation rate (\(da/dt\)) follows the film-rupture / slip-dissolution model:

$$\frac{da}{dt} = A \cdot (K_I)^n \quad \text{where} \quad K_I = Y \cdot \sigma_h \sqrt{\pi a}$$

Crack extension arrests only when the stress intensity factor falls below the environmental threshold: \(K_I < K_{ISCC}\).

4. In-Situ Non-Destructive Inspection Framework

Periodic plant turnaround inspections leave systems vulnerable to mid-cycle pinhole penetration. The integrity regime integrates two continuous screening modalities: 

+------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|             ADVANCED DUAL-TIER NDE MONITORING SUITE                     |
|                                                                        |
|  [ Magnetostrictive Guided Wave UT (GWUT) ]                            |
|  • 100% Volumetric Long-Range Screening (Up to 30 m from single collar)|
|  • Identifies Scalloped Wall Thinning and Internal FAC Clusters        |
|                                                                        |
|  [ Pulsed Eddy Current (PEC) Surface Matrix ]                          |
|  • Through-Insulation Inspection (No cladding strip-down required)    |
|  • Measures Remaining Ligament Thickness & Detects External CISCC Pits |
+------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  1. Guided Wave Ultrasonic Testing (GWUT): Torsional mode waves (\(T(0,1)\)) transmitted along pipe walls at \(20\text{--}100\text{ kHz}\) screen long linear runs, identifying cross-sectional metal loss \(\ge 3\%\) without stripping external coatings.
  2. Pulsed Eddy Current (PEC): Induces transient electromagnetic decay through thermal jacketing, measuring remaining ligament thickness with \(\pm 0.2\text{ mm}\) accuracy without requiring line shutdowns.

5. Conclusion

Operating critical energy and cooling networks in coastal maritime zones requires active defense against coupled internal and external corrosion mechanisms. By combining hydrodynamic wall shear modeling, chromium-stabilized metallurgy, and dual-tier ultrasonic/electromagnetic condition monitoring, engineering authorities eliminate unpredicted pressure boundary breaches, guaranteeing multi-decadal asset integrity.
