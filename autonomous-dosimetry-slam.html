Applied Nuclear Physics & Autonomous Robotics

Inverse Gamma Radiometry and Autonomous Robotic Dosimetry in High-Gradient Radiation Fields

Berkeley Vincent • Nuclear Physics, Radiological Protection & Autonomous Systems Architecture
Executive Summary: Characterizing complex nuclear environments—such as reactor cavities, spent fuel infrastructure, and heavily shielded containment penetrations—traditionally requires manual health physics entries that accumulate collective occupational dose. In high-gradient gamma fields (\(>50\text{ mSv/h}\)), static point surveys fail to capture localized hot-spot streaming or geometric attenuation boundaries. This paper develops an algorithmic framework for autonomous robotic survey platforms, coupling directional scintillation spectrometry with 3D LiDAR SLAM and Bayesian Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) inverse solvers to localize radiation sources, optimize ALARA, and eliminate direct personnel risk.

1. The Challenge of High-Gradient Dose Mapping & ALARA Limits

In nuclear plant sustainment, major outages, and decommissioning, occupational dose is strictly governed by the international ALARA principle (As Low As Reasonably Achievable). Conventional manual survey workflows confront three fundamental physical and operational limitations:

  1. Integrated Worker Exposure: Survey teams carrying hand-held ion chambers or GM meters must physically traverse uncharacterized fields, absorbing unnecessary transit dose before diagnostic measurements can be taken.
  2. The High-Gradient Inverse Problem: Dose rates near unshielded pipe elbows, valve bonnets, or filter banks can surge from \(0.05\text{ mSv/h}\) to over \(100\text{ mSv/h}\) across distances under \(0.5\text{ m}\). Discrete manual sampling frequently misses narrow streaming beams due to spatial interpolation gaps.
  3. Spectral & Directional Ambiguity: Standard survey meters record gross ambient dose equivalent rate (\(\dot{H}^*(10)\)) but lack directional sensitivity and real-time isotope identification. This obscures whether a hot spot originates from primary activation products (such as \(^{60}\text{Co}\) at \(1.17\text{ MeV}\) and \(1.33\text{ MeV}\)) or lower-energy scattered flux.

2. Mathematical Formulations & Inverse Radiometry

Forward Attenuated Photon Transport Formulation

For an isotropic gamma emitter of activity \(A_0\) located at unknown coordinates \(\mathbf{r}_s = (x_s, y_s, z_s)\), the uncollided photon flux (\(\Phi\)) recorded by an autonomous detector at position \(\mathbf{r}_d = (x_d, y_d, z_d)\) through multi-layer shielding media is modeled as:

$$\Phi(\mathbf{r}_d) = \left[ \frac{A_0 \cdot \Gamma_E}{4\pi \|\mathbf{r}_s - \mathbf{r}_d\|^2} \right] \cdot B(E, \mu, x) \cdot \exp\left( -\sum_{i} \mu_i x_i \right)$$

Where \(\Gamma_E\) is the specific gamma-ray emission yield, \(\mu_i\) is the linear attenuation coefficient of shielding layer \(i\), \(x_i\) is the ray-traced chord length through that material derived from the real-time onboard LiDAR mesh, and \(B(E, \mu, x)\) is the Taylor or Berger secondary buildup factor accounting for forward Compton scattering:

$$B(E, \mu, x) = 1 + a(\mu x) \cdot \exp\left(b \cdot \mu x\right)$$

Bayesian Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) Source Estimation

As the autonomous platform navigates an unmapped compartment, it records a time-series of \(N\) spatial poses and corresponding spectral count rates:

$$\mathcal{Z} = \{ (\mathbf{r}_1, C_1), (\mathbf{r}_2, C_2), \dots, (\mathbf{r}_N, C_N) \}$$

Because gamma emission and detection follow Poisson statistics, the likelihood of observing count rate \(C_k\) given candidate source position \(\mathbf{r}_s\) and activity \(A\) is:

$$P(C_k \mid \mathbf{r}_s, A) = \frac{(\lambda_k)^{C_k} e^{-\lambda_k}}{C_k!} \quad \text{where} \quad \lambda_k = \epsilon \cdot \Phi(\mathbf{r}_k, \mathbf{r}_s, A) + C_{\text{bkg}}$$

Source coordinates and source strength are resolved by minimizing the penalized negative log-posterior objective function:

$$J(\mathbf{r}_s, A) = \sum_{k=1}^N \left[ \lambda_k - C_k \ln(\lambda_k) \right] + \frac{1}{2\sigma_{\text{prior}}^2} \|\mathbf{r}_s - \mathbf{r}_{\text{prior}}\|^2$$

Applying iterative Gauss-Newton optimization yields sub-decimeter localization of concealed radiation sources behind bulkheads or valve internals prior to any human entry.

  1. Pose Estimation via LiDAR SLAM: In GPS-denied, metal-dense enclosures, a multi-beam 3D LiDAR sensor fused with an industrial tactical-grade IMU and wheel odometry delivers real-time trajectory estimation with \(<2\text{ cm}\) drift.
  2. Volumetric Ray-Casting over Voxel Grids: The platform dynamically processes raw point clouds into an OctoMap 3D voxel representation. Ray-casting algorithms extract material chord lengths (\(x_i\)) along the lines of sight between hypothesized source locations and the detector, feeding the exponential attenuation terms in real time.
  3. Automated Energy Windowing: Utilizing room-temperature Cadmium Zinc Telluride (CZT) coplanar grid sensors, the system performs automated pulse-height analysis (PHA). By windowing the \(1.17\text{ MeV}\) and \(1.33\text{ MeV}\) photopeaks of \(^{60}\text{Co}\) and the \(662\text{ keV}\) line of \(^{137}\text{Cs}\), the robot separates activation hot spots from background scatter.

5. Conclusion

Transitioning from manual, point-based radiological surveys to autonomous robotic dosimetry eliminates the classic compromise between personnel radiation exposure and spatial data resolution. Coupling classical photon transport attenuation models with Bayesian maximum a posteriori optimization and LiDAR SLAM allows industrial nuclear facilities to map radiation fields with high precision, minimize collective occupational dose, and enforce verifiable ALARA compliance across complex operating environments.
